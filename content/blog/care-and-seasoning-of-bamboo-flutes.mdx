# Care and Seasoning of Bamboo Flutes

A well-made bansuri, cared for properly, will outlive its player. Bamboo is a living material — it breathes, expands with humidity, and contracts in dry heat. A little attention, repeated consistently, keeps your flute in tune and crack-free for decades.

## Why bamboo cracks

Almost every cracked bansuri is a story about moisture. Your breath is warm and wet; the outside air is often cool and dry. When you play, the inner bore swells slightly while the outer surface stays dry. Over weeks and months, this creates stress that eventually splits the bamboo — usually as a hairline from the mouth hole running toward one end.

The fix is to keep the inner and outer walls in equilibrium. We do this with oil.

## Oiling: what, when, how

Use **pure mustard oil** or **almond oil**. Do not use mineral oil, vegetable oils that go rancid (olive, sunflower), or linseed oil (which hardens).

The simple routine:

1. Soak a piece of soft cotton cloth in mustard oil — wet, not dripping.
2. Thread it through the bore with a cleaning rod, or tie it to a string and pull it through.
3. Let the flute rest horizontally on a cloth overnight.
4. Wipe the outside with a lightly oiled cloth.

**Frequency:**

- A new flute: once a week for the first month.
- An established flute: once a month, or whenever you notice the bore looking pale or dry.
- In dry winter or AC environments: more often.
- In humid monsoon: less often.

## Daily habits that matter

- **Always swab after playing.** A thin cloth pulled through the bore removes moisture and prevents mold.
- **Never leave a flute in direct sunlight or near a heater.** Bamboo dries unevenly and cracks within hours.
- **Never store a flute in a sealed plastic bag.** Bamboo needs to breathe. Use a cotton sleeve or a wooden/cardboard case.
- **Warm the flute before playing in cold weather.** Hold it under your arm for two minutes. Blowing warm breath into a cold flute is one of the top causes of cracks.

## Travel and transport

On long journeys, the biggest risks are temperature swings and impact. A padded cotton sleeve inside a hard case solves both. Avoid checking flutes as baggage on flights — cargo holds swing from ground heat to freezing cruise altitudes in minutes.

## Signs your flute needs attention

- The tone feels "tight" or resistant, even after warming up.
- The inner bore looks grey or pale instead of dark and waxy.
- You see fine, faint lines running along the grain.
- Humidity changes have shifted the tuning by more than a few cents.

Any of these calls for an oiling session and a few days of rest away from direct play.

## A longer view

Think of your bansuri the way a violinist thinks of their instrument, or a tabla player thinks of their puris: a partner that rewards attention in kind. Most flutes that survive 30 or 40 years of playing belong to people who quietly oiled them once a month, every month, and never rushed the warm-up.

Do that, and yours will too.
